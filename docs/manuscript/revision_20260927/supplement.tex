\section{Run-to-run repeats of the calibration pipeline}
\label{sec:S-repeats}

\subsection{Method}
The complete pipeline was executed three times with the multi-start seeds 101, 202 and 303, fixed in advance, with clinical inputs and all other settings held constant. The seed enters only through the scrambling of starting points 2--6; starting point 1 is deterministic. All three runs are reported.

\subsection{Result}
The three runs returned the same selected parameter set, so the pipeline is exactly reproducible under the seeds tested. The reason is visible in the multi-start record (Table~\ref{tab:S-multistart}): the clinically seeded starting point is deterministic and is not affected by the seed, and in all three runs it had the lowest RMSE among the starts with no mean-pressure or flow target above the optimizer's 10\% setting, which is the first criterion of the selection rule (main text, Section~2.7). The seed changes only the five scrambled starting points.

Variability therefore exists between starting points rather than between runs. Across the 18 starts, the RMSE of relative error ranged from 0.0909 to 0.1509. One scrambled start in run 303 reached 0.0909, below the selected value of 0.1047, but had one mean-pressure or flow target above 10\%, so the selection rule ranked it below the seeded start. The reported result is therefore not the lowest RMSE attained, but the result the pre-specified selection rule chose.

Because the selected result does not vary, the three repeats do not quantify uncertainty. The measurement-noise simulation of the main text is the analysis intended for that purpose.

\section{Practical identifiability}
\label{sec:S-identifiability}

\subsection{Method}
Practical identifiability was assessed at the calibrated operating point from the scaled sensitivity matrix~\cite{Pope2009,Olufsen2013}
\begin{equation}
S_{ij} = \frac{\partial y_i}{\partial \theta_j}\,\frac{\theta_j}{\sigma_i},
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_i$ the declared measurement uncertainty of target $i$. Parameters with small column norms are weakly constrained; pairs with $|\rho| \ge 0.9$ between columns were flagged as collinear. The condition number $\mathrm{cond}(S)$ was evaluated for every subset of size $k \le p$ and the best-conditioned subset at each size reported as a candidate reduced set~\cite{vanOsta2020}. Subsets were scored by linear algebra alone, without re-simulation or recalibration.

\subsection{Result}
With 9 governed targets and 7 fitted parameters the pre-closure problem has 2 degrees of freedom. At the calibrated operating point, $\mathrm{cond}(S)$ of the full seven-parameter set was 61.9, identical in all three runs. No parameter pair was collinear at the $|\rho| \ge 0.9$ criterion; the strongest correlation was $-0.73$ between the pulmonary resistance scale and $C_{\mathrm{SAR}}$, followed by $+0.51$ between the systemic resistance scale and $R_{\mathrm{SVEN}}$. The weakest column was $C_d$ (norm 3.72), followed by $R_{\mathrm{SVEN}}$ (6.56); the pulmonary resistance scale was the best-constrained (27.6).

Conditioning improved sharply on removal of one parameter (Table~\ref{tab:S-cond}): the best six-parameter subset, which drops $R_{\mathrm{SVEN}}$, had $\mathrm{cond}(S) = 9.68$. The seven-parameter set retained here is therefore the least well-conditioned of the candidates, and is kept because it is the set the screen selected, not because it is the best conditioned. The reduced subsets were scored by linear algebra alone and were not recalibrated; they are reported as candidates for a future run.

\section{Objective function}
\label{sec:S-weights}
This section defines every term of the objective function of the main text (Section ``Objective function'') as implemented, with the values used in the reported calibration. $\varepsilon_k = (y_k - y_k^{\mathrm{clin}})/y_k^{\mathrm{clin}}$ is the signed relative error, $\lvert\varepsilon_k\rvert$ its magnitude, and $z_k = (y_k - y_k^{\mathrm{clin}})/\sigma_k$ the standardized residual with the declared uncertainty $\sigma_k$ (main text, Section ``Comparison between model and measurements'').

\paragraph{Target terms.} $\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}} w_k(\varepsilon_k/0.05)^2 + \sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}} w_k(\varepsilon_k/0.10)^2$, with the weights $w_k$ of Table~\ref{tab:S-weights}. Mean RA pressure, mean arterial pressure and $Q_s$ are not in these sums; they enter $J_{\mathrm{sys}}$.

\paragraph{Coupled systemic term.}
\begin{equation*}
J_{\mathrm{sys}} = 1.45\,z_{Q_s}^2 + 1.35\,z_{\overline{P}}^2 + 0.65\,z_{\mathrm{RA}}^2
+ 2.10\,d^2 + 3.00\,f^2 + 3.00\,f^2 + 0.40\left(\frac{Q_{\mathrm{AV}} - Q_s}{\sigma_{Q_s}}\right)^2,
\end{equation*}
where $d = \max(0, -z_{Q_s})$ is the one-sided deficit of $Q_s$, $f = \max[0, (Q_s^{\mathrm{clin}} - \sigma_{Q_s} - Q_s)/\max(0.5\sigma_{Q_s}, 0.05\,Q_s^{\mathrm{clin}})]$ is the deficit below one standard deviation, and $Q_{\mathrm{AV}}$ is the cycle-mean aortic valve flow. SVR enters with zero weight because it is a derived quantity.

\paragraph{Pressure-shape term.} $J_{\mathrm{shape}} = 0.8(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RFA,sys}}/0.10)^2 + 0.6(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RFA,dia}}/0.10)^2 + 0.8(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RFA,pulse}}/0.10)^2 + 0.8(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA,pulse}}/0.10)^2$, with the pulse pressure defined as systolic minus diastolic pressure.

\paragraph{Shunt term.} $J_{\mathrm{shunt}} = 0.65\left[(e_1/0.10)^2 + 0.25(e_2/0.10)^2\right]$, where $e_1 = \lvert \overline{Q}_{\mathrm{VSD}} - (Q_p - Q_s)\rvert / Q_{\mathrm{shunt}}^{\mathrm{clin}}$ checks that the cycle-mean orifice flow equals the model's $Q_p - Q_s$, and $e_2 = \lvert (Q_p - Q_s) - Q_{\mathrm{shunt}}^{\mathrm{clin}}\rvert / Q_{\mathrm{shunt}}^{\mathrm{clin}}$.

\paragraph{Guard term.} For each calibration target, $J_{\mathrm{guard}}$ adds $35[\max(0, \lvert\varepsilon_k\rvert - 0.12)/0.12]^2$ for systolic and diastolic RFA and PA pressure and mean PA pressure, and $55[\max(0, \lvert\varepsilon_k\rvert - 0.08)/0.08]^2$ for mean PA pressure.

\paragraph{Gate term.} $J_{\mathrm{gate}} = 20\sum_{k}[\max(0, \lvert\varepsilon_k\rvert - 0.10)/0.10]^2$ over the nine calibration targets.

\paragraph{Prior term.} $J_{\mathrm{prior}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{p}}\sum_j c_j \ln^2(x_j/x_j^{\mathrm{s}})$, with $c_j = 2.0$ for $C_{\mathrm{SAR}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{PAR}}$, $c_j = 1.0$ for the other parameters, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{p}} = 0.375$ (2.25 in stage E).

\paragraph{Bound term.} $J_{\mathrm{bound}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{b}}\sum_j\left\{[\max(0, 0.10 - d_j^{\mathrm{lo}})]^2 + [\max(0, 0.10 - d_j^{\mathrm{up}})]^2\right\}$, where $d_j^{\mathrm{lo}}$ and $d_j^{\mathrm{up}}$ are the distances of $x_j$ to its lower and upper bound as fractions of the bound interval, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{b}} = 17$ (120 in stage E).

\paragraph{Range term.} For each output without a clinical value, $J_{\mathrm{range}}$ adds $50(e/w)^2$ when the output leaves its soft range and $400(e/w)^2$ when it leaves its hard range, where $e$ is the distance outside the range and $w$ the width of the range (Table~\ref{tab:S-ranges}). A simulation that does not reach steady state adds $10^3$, and a simulation that fails adds a penalty of the same size.
